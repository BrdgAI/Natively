# Interview Facts :

1. All google questions are different from the Leetcode, they might be up to 80% similar in pattern match, but completely different in draft. 
2. The problem won’t be given a complete form, it may be vague and you have to form the complete problem by asking the missing items around it  — Edge cases need to be explored.
3. It would be followed by the follow up questions, and implementations of the follow ups. 
4. It is not an interview – it is a pair programming task. 
5. They just don’t want the right answer, they want to listen to your thought process and journey from the question building till reaching the answer. 
6. The interview is a conversation. Frame your thoughts clearly
7. Space and time complexity will be asked throughout. 
8. Google emphasizes conceptual understanding and problem-solving process (e.g., explaining trade-offs, optimizing)
9. The interviewer might just present the question verbally, and there might not be any question in the display. 
10. Follow up questions are guaranteed to be asked, and they’re pre-set for the questions(so don’t have to expect any variable difficulty). They will grade me on rubric on whether I was able to take the followups within the time, and whether I was able to solve the follow ups

# What to do in Interview :

1. Thinking and speaking out loud the entire time. Explain your thought process, from brute force to optimization
2. Being able to talk through and explain your thought process 
3. Slowly build the solution 
4. Have you fundamentals right
5. Look for the edge cases, present them, handle them.
6. At the end, pitch a dry run voluntarily to display you got it right.
